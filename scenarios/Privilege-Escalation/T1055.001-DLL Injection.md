# T1055 · C1 — Remote Thread Injection: The Signal Is Where the Thread Starts, Not What It's Called


## 1. Where C1 Sits

`.001` DLL Injection and `.002` PE Injection are different adversary methods but land on the **same telemetry primitive**: a thread created inside a foreign process. One detection covers both, which is the whole reason T1055 is organised by primitive rather than by sub-technique.

| Cluster | Subs | Primitive | Ceiling | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 Remote thread** | **.001, .002** | **Sysmon EID 8 CreateRemoteThread** | **0.85** | **this file** |
| C2 Thread-less | .003, .004 | EID 8 blind — EID 10 access rights only | 0.5 | pending |
| C3 Image replacement | .012, .013 | Sysmon EID 25 + EID 1 | 0.8 | separate file |
| C4 Linux/macOS | .008, .009, .014 | auditd ptrace / `task_for_pid` | 0.7 | out of scope |

---

## 2. Scenario and Detection Thesis

A process opens a handle to another process, writes code into its address space, and starts a thread to execute it. Sysmon logs the final step as EID 8, carrying the fields that matter: `SourceImage → TargetImage`, `StartAddress`, `StartModule`, `StartFunction`.

> **The attacker controls the name, the path, and the file on disk. The attacker does not control where the injected thread begins.** A source binary can be renamed `svchost.exe`, the DLL can be kept off disk entirely, the target can be a signed Microsoft process — but a reflectively-loaded thread still starts in memory that belongs to no loaded module. C1 keys on **thread origin**, because identity is spoofable and origin is the technique's physics.

The rename test in §7 exists to prove exactly that claim, not to decorate the validation table.

---

## 3. Two Signatures, One Cluster

The lab produced both injection styles against `notepad.exe`. They leave **inverse** traces, which is why a single-condition rule fails:

| Field | Reflective (`inject.exe`) | LoadLibrary (`dll-injection.exe`) |
|---|---|---|
| `StartModule` | **empty (`-`)** | `KERNEL32.DLL` |
| `StartFunction` | **empty** | `LoadLibraryW` |
| `StartAddress` | `0x000002084F…` — private / heap | `0x00007FF8…` — module-backed |
| Source → Target | cross-process | cross-process |

**Reflective injection** maps the DLL manually — no `LoadLibrary` call — so the thread begins at a private address backed by no module. `StartModule` is empty, and **that emptiness is the signal**.

**LoadLibrary injection** is the classic path: `CreateRemoteThread` pointed at `LoadLibraryW` in kernel32. `StartModule` is fully populated and `StartFunction` names the call. Here the signal is the **function**, not the emptiness.

A rule checking only `StartModule == empty` misses LoadLibrary injection. A rule checking only `StartFunction == LoadLibrary*` misses reflective. **Both arms are mandatory**, and they are the two branches in §5.

## 4. Baseline — Legitimate Remote Threads Are Real, and One Is Dangerous

An EID 8 baseline on the idle host was **not clean**. Two benign profiles appeared:

| Source → Target | StartModule | StartAddress | What it is | Removed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Code.exe → Code.exe` (×5) | `Code.exe` | `0x00007FF7E7AA8910` | VS Code — Electron/Chromium self-thread | `is_cross` |
| `dwm.exe → csrss.exe` | `-` | `0xFFFFF8007010D130` | kernel-space system thread | `is_kernel` |

**VS Code creates remote threads constantly** — every Electron/Chromium app does, as do Discord, Slack and Chrome. But source equals target: it injects into *itself*. Cross-process is the discriminator, and it costs one condition.

**`dwm.exe → csrss.exe` is the dangerous one.** It *is* cross-process, and its `StartModule` *is* empty — it matches the reflective arm on both counts. What saves the rule is the address: `0xFFFFF800…` is **kernel space**, and a user-mode reflective injection cannot start there. The address range itself is the filter.

Neither case needed an allowlist. Both were removed **behaviourally** — self-injection by `is_cross`, kernel threads by `is_kernel`. This is a stronger position than C3, where Azure Arc required a static path allowlist. **Static lists are a last resort; every entry is a bypass surface.** C1's allowlist is currently empty, and that is the design intent, not an oversight.

## 5. Scoring Model

Framework: `risk = impact × confidence`. Impact **60** (evasion + trust inheritance). C1 ceiling **0.85**.

| detect_type | Signal | Confidence | Risk | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SUSPICIOUS_UNBACKED_THREAD` | empty StartModule + cross-process + user-space + non-System32 source | 0.85 | **51** | High |
| `LOADLIB_INJECTION` | `StartFunction` ∈ {`LoadLibraryA`, `LoadLibraryW`} + cross-process | 0.75 | **45** | Medium |

**On the word "confidence."** It is a term in `impact × confidence`, but it is **not a calibrated probability** — nothing here measured that 85% of unbacked cross-process threads are malicious. It measures **how specific the signal is to the technique**. The product is a prioritisation score, not a Bayesian likelihood.

That distinction is not pedantic; it is the same error the detect_type name originally made. The reflective arm was first called `REFLECTIVE_INJECTION`, which states a **conclusion**. What the sensor actually reports is that *the thread's start address did not resolve to a loaded module* — suspicious, but also produced by JIT runtimes and some packers. Renamed to **`SUSPICIOUS_UNBACKED_THREAD`**: the output now names what was observed, not what it might mean.

> **An empty StartModule is a signal, not proof.** The alert name should survive being wrong.

## 6. The Rule

Both arms in one pass over EID 8 — no correlation, no lookup, no allowlist.

### Splunk (SPL)

```spl
index=windows_sysmon EventCode=8
| eval src=lower(SourceImage), tgt=lower(TargetImage)
| eval is_cross  = if(src!=tgt, 1, 0)
| eval is_kernel = if(match(StartAddress,"(?i)^0xffff"), 1, 0)
| eval src_sys   = if(match(src,"^c:\\\\windows\\\\system32\\\\"), 1, 0)
| eval sm_empty  = if(StartModule=="-" OR StartModule=="" OR isnull(StartModule), 1, 0)
| eval sig_reflective = if(sm_empty==1 AND is_cross==1 AND is_kernel==0 AND src_sys==0, 1, 0)
| eval sig_loadlib    = if(StartFunction IN ("LoadLibraryA","LoadLibraryW") AND is_cross==1, 1, 0)
| where sig_reflective==1 OR sig_loadlib==1
| eval detect_type = case(sig_reflective==1, "REFLECTIVE_INJECTION",
                          sig_loadlib==1,    "LOADLIB_INJECTION")
| eval confidence  = case(sig_reflective==1, 0.85, sig_loadlib==1, 0.75, 1==1, 0.50)
| eval impact = 60
| eval risk_score = impact * confidence
| where risk_score >= 30
| table _time, detect_type, SourceImage, TargetImage, StartFunction, StartModule,
        StartAddress, ProcessGuid, TargetProcessGuid, confidence, risk_score
| sort - risk_score, - _time
```

### Sentinel (KQL)

```kql
let lookback = 24h;
Sysmon
| where TimeGenerated > ago(lookback)
| where EventID == 8
| extend src = tolower(SourceImage), tgt = tolower(TargetImage)
| extend is_cross  = iff(src != tgt, 1, 0)
| extend is_kernel = iff(StartAddress startswith "0xFFFF" or StartAddress startswith "0xffff", 1, 0)
| extend src_sys   = iff(src startswith @"c:\windows\system32\", 1, 0)
| extend sm_empty  = iff(StartModule == "-" or isempty(StartModule), 1, 0)
| extend sig_reflective = iff(sm_empty == 1 and is_cross == 1 and is_kernel == 0 and src_sys == 0, 1, 0)
| extend sig_loadlib    = iff(StartFunction in ("LoadLibraryA","LoadLibraryW") and is_cross == 1, 1, 0)
| where sig_reflective == 1 or sig_loadlib == 1
| extend detect_type = case(sig_reflective == 1, "SUSPICIOUS_UNBACKED_THREAD",
                            sig_loadlib == 1,    "LOADLIB_INJECTION", "")
| extend confidence = case(sig_reflective == 1, 0.85, sig_loadlib == 1, 0.75, 0.50)
| extend impact = 60
| extend risk_score = impact * confidence
| where risk_score >= 30
| extend Severity = case(risk_score >= 50, "High", risk_score >= 40, "Medium", "Low")
| project TimeGenerated, detect_type, Severity, risk_score, confidence,
          SourceImage, TargetImage, StartFunction, StartModule, StartAddress,
          SourceProcessGuid, TargetProcessGuid
| order by risk_score desc, TimeGenerated desc
```

### Known gaps

Documented rather than silently patched — the figures in §7 were produced by these exact queries.

| # | Gap | Effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The `SUSPICIOUS_UNBACKED_THREAD` rename landed in KQL only.** SPL still emits `REFLECTIVE_INJECTION` | The two platforms disagree on the alert name for the same event, and the Splunk name still states a conclusion the sensor cannot support (§5) | Rename in SPL |
| 2 | **SPL projects `ProcessGuid`, which does not exist on EID 8.** The field is `SourceProcessGuid` | The column is empty. This breaks RBA `SourceProcessGuid` dedup *and* the EID 10 → EID 8 correlation planned in §9, both of which key on the source process | Replace with `SourceProcessGuid` |
| 3 | **SPL has no `collect`, no `risk_object`, no `detection_id`.** KQL has entity mapping and Severity | SPL is a search, not a feeder — it cannot write to `index=risk` | Add the standard feeder tail, scan window equal to the cron interval |
| 4 | **`where risk_score >= 30` is dead code in both.** The lowest reachable score is 45 | Harmless, but it implies a tier that does not exist | Drop it, or introduce a real low tier |
| 5 | **The `0.50` fallback in `confidence` is unreachable** — the preceding `where` guarantees one arm matched | Dead branch | Drop it |

Gaps 1 and 2 are the substantive ones. Gap 2 in particular is invisible in testing — the rule fires correctly, the score is right, and only the empty column reveals it. It would be found in production the day someone tried to build the correlation chain on top.

## 7. Validation

Six events, all classified correctly, **identical across Splunk and Sentinel** — five alerts each, same scores.

| Test | detect_type | Risk | Severity | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `inject.exe → notepad.exe` (×3) | unbacked thread | **51** | High | injection — alerts |
| `dll-injection.exe → notepad.exe` | `LOADLIB_INJECTION` | **45** | Medium | injection — alerts |
| `svchost.exe → notepad.exe` (renamed binary) | `LOADLIB_INJECTION` | **45** | Medium | **alerts despite the name** |
| `Code.exe → Code.exe` (×5) | — | — | — | benign self-thread — silent |
| `dwm.exe → csrss.exe` | — | — | — | kernel thread — silent |

**The `svchost.exe` row is the proof of the thesis.** The same `dll-injection.exe` binary was renamed to a legitimate system process name and re-run. It alerted identically, because the rule never looked at the name — it looked at `StartFunction = LoadLibraryW` plus cross-process. **Identity spoofing did not evade the rule.** Rename it to anything; the behaviour is unchanged, so the detection is unchanged.

The two benign profiles stayed silent with **no allowlist entries at all** — structure alone separated them.

## 8. What This Cannot Catch

C1 is a **single-event rule on EID 8**, and its blindness is exactly bounded by that:

| Evades C1 | Why | Covered by |
|---|---|---|
| APC injection (`QueueUserAPC`) | no thread created → **no EID 8 at all** | C2 (EID 10) |
| Thread hijacking (`SetThreadContext`) | no thread created → **no EID 8 at all** | C2 (EID 10) |
| `mavinject.exe /INJECTRUNNING` | injects via `NtSetInformationProcess`, not `CreateRemoteThread` | LOLBin rule (T1218) |
| Process hollowing | image replaced, no remote thread into a foreign process | C3 (EID 25) |

If the attacker changes the **execution primitive** — abandons `CreateRemoteThread` for an APC or a hijacked thread — EID 8 is never emitted and C1 sees nothing at all. This is not a flaw in the rule; it is the boundary of the cluster and the reason C2 exists.

**One guard is a known bypass.** The reflective arm excludes System32 sources (`src_sys`) to drop the `dwm → csrss` bleed-through and the powershell-hollow noise from the C3 lab. A malicious binary planted in System32 walks straight through it. This is **false-positive reduction, not a security control** — the proper fix correlates EID 7 (ImageLoad) signature data, distinguishing a signed system binary from an unsigned dropper, which EID 8 alone does not carry.

## 9. The Next Layer — Why Single-Event Is Only a First Layer

C1 answers "was a suspicious remote thread created?" A stronger detection answers a **behavioural chain**: did the same source process first *open* the target with injection rights, and *then* create the unbacked thread?

```
EID 10  ProcessAccess: A → B, GrantedAccess includes VM_WRITE | CREATE_THREAD
            │  same SourceProcessGuid
EID 8   CreateRemoteThread: A → B, StartModule empty
            ↓
        correlated score >> either signal alone
```

Alone, an unbacked EID 8 thread can be a JIT runtime. Alone, an EID 10 `OpenProcess` is enormous noise. But the **same source process** opening a target with `VM_WRITE|CREATE_THREAD` and then starting an unbacked thread inside it is the full injection signature — two weak signals that are strong together.

This chain is built in **C2**, where EID 10 / GrantedAccess enters as the primary sensor. C1 is not replaced by it; C1 is the remote-thread layer the chain stands on. Note that the chain keys on `SourceProcessGuid` — the field gap 2 in §6 currently leaves empty on the Splunk side.

## 11. Limitations & Production Readiness

**Validated.** Two-signature detection (reflective + LoadLibrary) on real EID 8 telemetry. Behavioural false-positive removal — self-injection and kernel-space threads — with **no allowlist required**. Identity-spoofing resistance proven by the `svchost.exe` rename test. Full Splunk ↔ Sentinel parity: five alerts each, identical scores.

**Not production-ready:**

- **Single event, no correlation.** Coverage is narrow by design — EID 8 only. The EID 10 → EID 8 chain (§9) is the intended next layer.
- **`src_sys` guard is bypassable.** A System32-resident dropper evades the reflective arm; needs EID 7 signature correlation to fix properly.
- **C3 bleed-through.** The powershell → `<unknown>` hollowing events from the C3 lab also match C1's shape. They are filtered by `src_sys` here, but the correct treatment is `SourceProcessGuid` dedup at the RBA aggregation layer, since that activity maps to its own technique — and that dedup is blocked by gap 2 in §6.
- **Five open gaps between SPL and KQL** (§6), including the detect_type rename and the wrong GUID field.
- **`confidence` is signal-specificity, not probability** (§5) — never calibrated against a real malicious/benign base rate.
- **Single host, manual trigger.** A fleet carries benign cross-process injectors this lab lacks — EDR agents, accessibility tooling, IME. Those are the case the currently-empty allowlist exists for, and the first thing a real baseline would surface.

**Next:** close gaps 1–3; then **C2** — generate APC injection to confirm EID 8's blindness first-hand, then build the EID 10 / GrantedAccess correlation chain this rule sets up.
